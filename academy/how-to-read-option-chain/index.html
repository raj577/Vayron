How to read an option chain, step by step

An option chain is a table that lists every available option contract for one underlying, such as NIFTY, BANKNIFTY or a stock, for one expiry date. It looks overwhelming the first time, with dozens of columns and rows, but it follows a simple layout. Once you know where to look, it becomes one of the most informative screens in the market.

The basic layout

  • Strike prices run down the middle. Each row is one strike, for example 24,000, 24,050, 24,100.
  • Call options (CE) are on the left and put options (PE) are on the right of the strike column.
  • The current price of the underlying sits somewhere in the middle of the list. The strike closest to it is called the at-the-money (ATM) strike.

ITM, ATM and OTM

Every strike is either in, at or out of the money:

  • A call is in the money (ITM) when its strike is below the current price, and out of the money (OTM) when it is above.
  • A put is in the money when its strike is above the current price, and out of the money when it is below.

Many option chains shade the ITM side so you can see the dividing line at a glance. OTM options are cheaper because they have no intrinsic value, only time value. That is also why most of them expire worthless.

The columns that matter

  • LTP (last traded price): the premium of the option per unit. Multiply by the lot size to get the cost of one contract.
  • Change / % change: how the premium moved versus the previous close.
  • OI (open interest): how many contracts are open at that strike. See what open interest is.
  • Change in OI: contracts added or removed today. This shows where fresh positions are being built.
  • Volume: contracts traded today. High volume tells you where activity is concentrated.
  • IV (implied volatility): the volatility the option price implies. Higher IV means more expensive options. See implied volatility explained.
  • Bid and ask: the best price buyers are offering and sellers are asking. A wide gap means the option is illiquid and costly to trade.

A simple way to read it

  1. Find the ATM strike. Start from the row nearest the current price.
  2. Look for the largest call OI above the price. Big call OI is created mostly by call writers, who profit if the price stays below that strike. Traders often read it as a resistance zone.
  3. Look for the largest put OI below the price. Put writers profit if the price stays above that strike, so big put OI is often read as a support zone.
  4. Check change in OI. If writers keep adding calls at a lower strike during the day, the "ceiling" is moving down. If they add puts at higher strikes, the "floor" is moving up.
  5. Compare the two sides. The put-call ratio summarises this balance in one number.

An illustrative example

Suppose an index trades near 24,050. The biggest call OI sits at 24,200 and the biggest put OI at 23,900. Many traders would read 23,900 to 24,200 as the range option writers expect for this expiry. If the price breaks above 24,200 and call OI there starts falling while put OI builds at 24,100, writers are shifting their positions, and the range is moving up. These are example numbers for learning, not real levels.

Common mistakes

  • Treating OI levels as guarantees. Writers adjust or exit when the market moves against them, and walls can disappear quickly.
  • Ignoring liquidity. Far OTM strikes may show cheap premiums but wide bid-ask spreads.
  • Mixing expiries. Each expiry has its own chain. The nearest expiry usually has the most activity but also the fastest time decay.
  • Buying cheap far-OTM options as "lottery tickets". Their low price reflects a low chance of finishing in the money.

Frequently asked questions

Which side of the option chain shows calls?

Calls (CE) are on the left of the strike column and puts (PE) are on the right on most Indian broker and NSE option chains.

What does high call OI at a strike mean?

It means many call contracts are open at that strike, mostly created by call writers. Traders often read it as a resistance zone, but writers can exit or shift if the price moves through it.

What is the ATM strike?

The at-the-money strike is the strike price closest to the current price of the underlying. It usually has the highest time value and a delta near 0.5.
