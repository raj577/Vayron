Order flow basics: bids, asks and who is in control

Charts show you where price has been. Order flow looks at the orders that actually move it: who is buying, who is selling, and how urgently. It is a closer, more detailed view of the same market, and it helps explain why a price level holds or breaks.

Market orders and limit orders

  • A limit order says "buy at â‚¹500 or lower" or "sell at â‚¹502 or higher". It waits in the order book until someone trades with it. Limit orders provide liquidity.
  • A market order says "buy or sell right now at the best available price". It removes liquidity from the book.

Price moves when market orders on one side use up the limit orders resting at the best price, forcing trades at the next level.

The bid, the ask and the spread

The bid is the highest price a buyer is currently willing to pay. The ask (or offer) is the lowest price a seller will accept. The difference is the spread. Liquid stocks and index futures have very tight spreads; illiquid ones have wide spreads, which quietly raise your trading cost.

Market depth

Market depth shows the quantity waiting at several price levels on each side. Most Indian broker feeds show the best five levels. Large quantities on the bid side can look like support and large quantities on the ask side like resistance. But orders can be cancelled at any time, so depth shows intent, not commitment.

Aggressive buyers and sellers

Every trade has a buyer and a seller, so order flow asks a different question: who crossed the spread?

  • A trade at the ask means a buyer was impatient and paid the seller's price. This counts as buy volume.
  • A trade at the bid means a seller was impatient and accepted the buyer's price. This counts as sell volume.

When exact bid/ask stamps are not available, tools classify trades by comparing each trade's price with the previous one (the "tick rule"). This is an approximation but works well on liquid instruments.

Delta and cumulative delta

Delta in order flow (not to be confused with option delta) is buy volume minus sell volume over a period, such as one five-minute candle. Cumulative delta adds these up over the session.

  • Rising price with rising cumulative delta suggests aggressive buyers are driving the move.
  • Rising price with flat or falling delta suggests the move is weak, perhaps driven by a lack of sellers rather than strong buyers.
  • Large selling into a level that does not break can mean a big buyer is absorbing it with limit orders.

Absorption and exhaustion

Absorption is when heavy aggressive volume hits a level but price barely moves, because passive orders on the other side keep filling it. Exhaustion is when a move continues but aggressive volume dries up. Both are common ideas in order flow analysis and often appear near support and resistance or order blocks.

Limitations

  • Depth can be "spoofed" with orders that are pulled before they trade.
  • Large participants split orders into many small ones, hiding their size.
  • Order flow is best for timing and context; on its own it does not predict where price will go.
  • Flow measured from a live feed only starts when you connect, unless earlier candles are backfilled from your broker.

Frequently asked questions

What is order flow in trading?

Order flow is the study of actual buy and sell orders, including who is trading aggressively at the bid or ask, to understand why prices move and whether a level is likely to hold.

What is cumulative delta?

It is the running total of buy volume minus sell volume through the session. It shows whether aggressive buyers or sellers have been more active overall.

Can market depth be trusted?

Only partly. Resting orders can be cancelled at any time and large traders split their orders, so depth shows intent rather than guaranteed support or resistance.
