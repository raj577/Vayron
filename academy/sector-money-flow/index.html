The index tells you whether the market as a whole is up or down. Sector money flow tells you where the money is going underneath. On a day when NIFTY barely moves, banks might be rising strongly while IT and metals fall. Watching sectors helps you understand what is really driving the market.

What "money flow" means

Money flow is an estimate, not a record of who bought what. A common approach combines traded value (price Ã— volume) with the direction of each trade or price move:

  • When a stock rises on heavy traded value, that value is counted as inflow.
  • When it falls on heavy traded value, that value is counted as outflow.
  • Adding this up across all stocks in a sector gives a net inflow or outflow for the sector.

Every trade has a buyer and a seller, so money does not literally "enter" a stock. What flow measures is which side was more aggressive, whether buyers were willing to pay up or sellers were willing to hit lower prices.

Sector rotation

Large investors such as mutual funds and foreign institutions move money between sectors as their view of the economy changes. This is called sector rotation. Common patterns that traders talk about include:

  • Money moving into banks, autos and capital goods when growth expectations improve.
  • Money moving into FMCG, pharma and other defensives when investors get cautious.
  • IT reacting to the rupee and to global technology spending.
  • Metals and energy following global commodity prices.

These are tendencies, not rules. Rotation is easiest to see over several sessions rather than a few minutes.

Why heavyweight sectors matter

Financial services carry the largest weight in NIFTY 50, followed by sectors such as IT, energy and consumer goods. A strong move in banks can lift the whole index even when most other stocks are falling. That is why many traders check BANKNIFTY alongside NIFTY, and why breadth matters.

Breadth: how many stocks are joining in

Breadth compares the number of advancing and declining stocks. A sector that is up 1% because two giants rallied, while most of its stocks fell, is weaker than a sector where most stocks are rising together. Healthy rallies usually show both inflow and broad participation.

How to read a sector flow dashboard

  1. Start with the market view: overall mood, breadth and net flow.
  2. Rank sectors: which have the biggest net inflow and outflow right now?
  3. Check consistency: has the leader been leading all day or did it just spike?
  4. Drill into stocks: within the strongest sector, which stocks carry the flow? Use order flow and RSI to look closer.
  5. Compare with relative strength: a sector outperforming NIFTY on a down day often shows genuine demand.

Limitations

  • Flow is an estimate built from prices and volumes; it does not reveal real buyer identity.
  • Block deals, index rebalancing and expiry-day activity can create large one-off flows.
  • Intraday flow can reverse quickly, so avoid reading too much into a few minutes.

Frequently asked questions

What is sector rotation?

Sector rotation is the shift of investor money from one group of stocks to another, for example from defensives such as FMCG into cyclicals such as banks and autos, as views on the economy change.

Does money flow show who is buying?

No. It is an estimate based on price and traded value. It shows which side was more aggressive, not the identity of buyers or sellers.

Why can NIFTY rise when most stocks fall?

Because NIFTY is weighted by company size. A strong move in a few heavyweights, especially in financial services, can lift the index even when breadth is weak.
