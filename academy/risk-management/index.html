Risk management for traders: protect your capital first

This lesson is for education only. It is not a recommendation to buy, sell or hold any security or derivative. Vayron is not a SEBI-registered investment adviser or research analyst. Any prices or levels below are made-up examples, not real market data. Trading in F&O involves substantial risk.

Every lesson in this Academy explains a way to read the market. This one is about something more important: staying in the game. No indicator, order block or option chain reading works every time. What separates traders who last from those who do not is usually how they manage losses, not how often they are right.

Start with the numbers

SEBI has studied individual traders in equity futures and options several times. Its studies found that roughly nine out of ten individual F&O traders lost money over the periods studied, and that transaction costs added significantly to those losses. That is not a reason to panic, but it is a reason to treat trading as a high-risk activity and to protect your capital before thinking about returns.

The 1% rule: position sizing

A simple and widely used guideline is to risk no more than 1% (or at most 2%) of your trading capital on any single trade. "Risk" here means how much you lose if your stop loss is hit, not the size of the position.

With 1% risk, ten losing trades in a row cost about 10% of capital, which is painful but survivable. With 10% risk per trade, the same streak can wipe out most of an account.

Stop losses

  • Decide where your idea is proven wrong before you enter, for example just beyond a support level or an order block.
  • Place the stop there, not at a random round number of rupees.
  • Do not widen a stop once the trade moves against you. Moving it closer to lock in profit is fine.
  • Remember that gaps and fast markets can fill you worse than your stop price.

Track both your win rate and your average win versus average loss; together they tell you whether a method works.

Leverage and F&O

Futures and options let you control a large position with a small amount of margin. That magnifies gains and losses equally. Selling options in particular can lead to losses many times the premium received. Before trading derivatives, know the full contract value, the maximum loss, and how it would feel to lose it. The Greeks and expiry day lessons explain why options can move so fast.

Daily and weekly loss limits

Set a maximum loss for the day, such as 3% of capital, and stop trading when you hit it. Losses often lead to "revenge trading", bigger and worse trades taken to win the money back. A hard limit breaks that cycle.

Costs add up

Brokerage, exchange charges, STT, stamp duty, GST and slippage are paid on every trade, win or lose. Frequent trading in small positions can turn a break-even method into a losing one. Include costs when you evaluate your results.

Keep a journal

Write down why you took each trade, where your stop and target were, what happened and how you felt. Over a few weeks, patterns appear: the setups that work for you, the times of day you trade badly, and the mistakes you repeat. A short checklist before any trade

  1. Where am I wrong, and where is my stop?
  2. How much will I lose if I am wrong, and is that within 1â€“2% of capital?
  3. What is my target, and is the risk-reward worth it after costs?
  4. Is there an event (results, policy, expiry) that could gap the price?
  5. Am I calm, or am I trying to win back a loss?

Frequently asked questions

What is the 1% rule in trading?

It is a guideline to risk no more than 1% of your trading capital on any single trade, where risk is the amount you lose if your stop loss is hit.

How do I calculate position size?

Divide the rupee amount you are willing to risk by the distance between your entry and your stop loss. The result is the number of shares or units to trade.

Why do most F&O traders lose money?

SEBI's studies point to high leverage, frequent trading, transaction costs and a lack of risk control. Option buyers also face time decay, while option sellers face rare but large losses.
