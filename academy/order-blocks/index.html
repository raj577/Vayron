An order block is a price zone where a large burst of buying or selling began, often just before a strong move. The idea comes from price action trading and is popular in what many traders call "smart money concepts". Traders watch these zones because price sometimes returns to them and reacts again.

The idea behind order blocks

Big institutions cannot buy or sell their full size at one price without moving the market against themselves. So they build positions over a range of prices. The theory says that the area where they accumulated, just before price takes off, may still have unfilled orders. When price comes back to that area, those orders can step in again and cause a reaction.

How a bullish order block is usually defined

  1. Price makes a sharp, strong move up, often breaking a recent swing high.
  2. Look at the last down (bearish) candle before that impulse began.
  3. The range of that candle, from its low to its high (or its body), is marked as the bullish order block.

A bearish order block is the mirror image: the last up candle before a strong move down that breaks a recent swing low.

What traders look for

  • A strong displacement: the move away from the zone should be fast and decisive, not a slow drift.
  • A break of structure: the move should take out a prior swing high or low, showing a genuine shift.
  • Freshness: a zone that has not been revisited yet is often considered stronger. Once price returns and trades through it, it is called "mitigated".
  • Confluence: zones that line up with support or resistance, round numbers or large option OI strikes get extra attention.

How order blocks relate to order flow

An order block is drawn from candles, so it is an inference about where large orders were. Order flow tools can add evidence: heavy buy delta inside the zone, or absorption of selling when price returns, supports the idea that buyers are active there. Without that confirmation, an order block is just a highlighted area on a chart.

Why order blocks are not guarantees

  • Different traders draw them differently (wicks vs bodies, which timeframe), so there is no single "correct" zone.
  • Many zones fail, especially against a strong trend or on news.
  • Hindsight makes them look better than they are; testing rules on past data honestly matters.
  • They work best as areas of interest combined with risk control, not as entry signals on their own. Read the risk management lesson before using any zone.

Frequently asked questions

What is an order block in trading?

It is a price zone, usually the last opposite candle before a strong move, where traders believe large orders were placed. Price sometimes reacts when it returns to that zone.

What is the difference between an order block and support?

Support is any level where buying has repeatedly appeared. An order block is a specific zone defined by the candle before a strong move. They often overlap, which traders call confluence.

Do order blocks always work?

No. Many order blocks fail, especially against strong trends or during news. They are areas to watch, not guaranteed reversal points.
